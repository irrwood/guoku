## Style Signals

- **Curation over search**: The voice assumes someone has already looked through the noisy marketplace and brought back one object worth noticing.
- **Magazine thinking**: Goods are handled like small editorial subjects, not SKU rows. Copy should feel selected and framed.
- **Discovery cost**: The writing should reduce the user's mental effort. One clear feeling is better than five complete selling points.
- **Taste projection**: The object says something about the person who keeps it, carries it, drinks from it, writes with it, or leaves it on a desk.
- **Useful plus interesting**: Guoku is not pure mood writing. The product should still feel usable, but utility is shown through a moment.
- **Concise product text**: Prefer short lines and a small number of observations. Do not over-explain why the object matters.
- **User review energy**: Let light praise, small complaints, and self-mockery coexist. This makes the copy human instead of institutional.

## Writing Moves

- Turn material into touch: glass becomes cold edges, canvas becomes softened corners, metal becomes morning weight.
- Turn function into a tiny ritual: storage becomes returning things to their place, noise cancellation becomes entering a smaller room.
- Turn usefulness into relief: a lamp does not "improve productivity"; it makes the desk less like a place for punishment.
- Turn taste into identity: the object belongs to someone who does not rush, who keeps receipts in books, or who buys one unnecessary but correct thing.
- Use time as atmosphere: late night, rain, weekday morning, Sunday after four, the first ten minutes after getting home.
- Let a small defect make the line warmer: too much trouble, a little expensive, not life-changing, but somehow still kept.

## Category Prompts

Use these category lenses when a product has few details.

- **Home goods**: quiet order, returning home, objects finding their place.
- **Kitchenware**: sound, temperature, small rituals, waiting.
- **Bags and apparel**: traces of use, understated self-image, weather and commute.
- **Digital products**: silence, focus, friction removed, the illusion of being organized.
- **Stationery**: unfinished plans, handwriting, desk light, seriousness mixed with self-mockery.
- **Food and drink**: late-night hunger, memory, warmth, small comfort without sentimentality.

## Anti-Patterns

- Do not convert the style into vague "高级感".
- Do not make every line lonely or rainy; the urban mood is seasoning, not the whole meal.
- Do not imitate ecommerce detail pages. Price, discount, parameter, and guarantee language should only appear if the user asks.
- Do not overuse Tokyo, cafe, bookstore, or convenience-store imagery. Prefer product-specific moments first.
- Do not quote Guoku or media articles as generated product copy.
